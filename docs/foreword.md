# Foreword

In 1997 I was working as a system administrator managing databases and big Unix
machines. Like every admin, I wanted to know that something was broken before
the phone rang. The tools available back then were either too expensive, too
rigid, or simply not good enough, so I started writing my own in the evenings.
Besides, I had loved programming since my early days: the magic of writing code
and watching a machine bring it to life.

I had no grand plan. I wanted something that would collect data reliably, notice
problems, and tell me about them. That small side project became Zabbix. In 2001
I released it to the world under an open source license, mostly out of curiosity
about whether anyone else would find it useful.

I did not expect what happened next.

People started using Zabbix. Then they started sending bug reports, patches,
translations, templates, and ideas. Some simply wrote to say “thank you.” Those
short messages meant more than their authors could imagine: in the early years,
when Zabbix was still something I built in my free time, they were a great
motivator for me to keep going. Some of them asked questions on the forum; a few
years later, the same people were answering questions for others. The software
grew, the company grew, but the thing that grew fastest was the community around
it.

Today Zabbix is a complete monitoring and observability solution, running
everywhere from a handful of servers in a small office to some of the largest
infrastructures in the world: banks, telecom operators, universities,
governments, factories. The small tool I once wrote for myself now competes head
to head with the most advanced systems on the market. It runs in places I will
never see, built and maintained by people I have never met. That still amazes
me.

None of this would have been possible without the Zabbix team. For many years,
our engineers, support specialists, trainers, and everyone else at Zabbix have
worked hard to turn a side project into software that people trust with their
most critical systems. Together with the community, they are the reason Zabbix
is what it is today.

I have always believed that open source is not only about access to code. It is
about access to knowledge, and about freedom in general. Free software that
nobody understands is not truly free. This is why a project like this book
matters so much to me.

The book you are holding was not written by Zabbix the company. It was written
by Zabbix users, people who deploy, tune, troubleshoot, and extend Zabbix in
real environments, often under real pressure. They gave their evenings and
weekends to put their hard-won experience on paper, and they chose to share it
openly, in the same spirit in which Zabbix itself was shared.

That is what makes this book different. Official documentation tells you what
each feature does. This book tells you how experienced practitioners actually
use those features: what works, what to avoid, and why. It covers the details
that usually live only in the heads of senior engineers or in long forum
threads, and it puts them in one place, in order. Zabbix is developing rapidly,
with every major release bringing new observability capabilities and new ways of
solving old problems. At this pace of change, a practical, up-to-date source of
knowledge like this one is extremely important.

To every author, reviewer, and contributor — thank you. You have done something
that no company could have done alone. I am proud that Zabbix has a community
capable of creating something like this.

If you are new to Zabbix, do not be intimidated by the size of this book. You do
not need to read it cover to cover. Start with the foundations, get your first
hosts and triggers working, and come back to the advanced chapters when your
environment, and your questions, grow. If you have used Zabbix for years, I am
confident you will still find something new here: a better way to design
templates, a cleaner approach to scaling, a trick that saves you an hour every
week. I learned a few LLD tricks while reading it.

Most of all, I hope this book helps you move from simply collecting data to
truly understanding your systems. That is what observability is really about,
and it has always been the point of Zabbix.

In the end, this is our mission. Every day we rely on services we rarely think
about: payments, hospitals, transport, communication, the power in our homes.
Behind each of them are people and systems making sure they are up and running,
fast and reliable. Monitoring and observability are a quiet part of that work,
but an essential one, and I truly believe that by helping people keep their
services healthy, Zabbix helps to make the world a little better.

And when you have learned something, share it. Answer a question on the forum,
publish a template, write a blog post, or contribute to the next edition of this
book. That is how Zabbix grew from one admin's side project into what it is
today, and that is how it will keep growing.

Welcome to the Zabbix community. Enjoy the book.

**Alexei Vladishev**

Founder and CEO of Zabbix

Riga, Latvia, November 2026
